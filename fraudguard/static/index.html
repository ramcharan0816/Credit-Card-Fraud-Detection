Inputs

V1–V28 are principal components of the original card data, anonymised by the dataset's publishers for confidentiality. Only Amount is a readable feature.

Why a Decision Tree?

It is fast, needs no GPU, and is easy to serve. Trade-off: a single tree can overfit, so ensembles are a natural next step.

Overall accuracy is 99.91%, but because fraud is only ~0.17% of transactions, accuracy alone is misleading. Fraud precision and recall are the numbers that matter: roughly 3 in 4 frauds are caught, and about 3 in 4 alerts are real. (Scoring on the full dataset gives 0.93/0.91, but that includes training rows and is over-optimistic.)

Limitations & responsible use

Screening aid only

Not a substitute for a bank's fraud team; flagged transactions need human review.

Dataset scope

Trained on European card data from a short period; patterns may not generalise to other regions or later fraud tactics.

Imbalanced data

Very few fraud examples limit what a single tree can learn. Expect false alarms and missed cases.
